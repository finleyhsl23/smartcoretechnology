Where it started

A Christmas Day idea,
in the Three Valleys

It was Christmas Day 2025. Finley Hassall was on a family skiing trip in the Three Valleys, France — one of the biggest ski areas in the world. Between runs, somewhere between the mountain air and a quiet conversation with family, the idea for SmartCore crystallised.

Not from a business plan. Not from a whiteboard session. From a genuine frustration: too many small and medium businesses were struggling with software that was either too expensive, too complicated, or simply built for someone else. The gap was obvious. The question was whether someone would actually fill it properly.

⛷️
Three Valleys, France — 25 December 2025

The same trip that gave us a family Christmas also gave us SmartCore. Finley came home with skis, a sunburn, and a proper plan. Work started almost immediately.

The person behind it

From gaming obsession
to building the real thing

Finley is from Derbyshire, in the Midlands. His relationship with technology didn't start in a classroom — it started with gaming. From an early age, he was the kind of person who had to have the newest machine, the fastest setup, the best specs. But what set him apart wasn't the hardware — it was the compulsion to understand how everything worked.

Where most people played games, Finley explored settings. He pushed limits, broke things, figured out why, and put them back together. That habit — curiosity turned into competence — is what eventually led him into web development. Not through a bootcamp or a university module. Through the same instinct that made him want to understand every option in every menu: he just kept going until he could build something real.

That background shapes how SmartCore is built. Every system is hand-crafted. Every flow has been thought through. Nothing is templated. Nothing is borrowed. If something exists in SmartCore, it's because it was designed, written, and tested from scratch.

Shaped by real business

A family that lives
and breathes business

Finley's parents run their own business together. Two of his siblings work for them. Business isn't something his family talks about around the dinner table occasionally — it's the dinner table conversation. Every evening. Always work, always operational, always the next thing that needs solving.

Growing up in that environment gave Finley something most software developers simply don't have: a genuine feel for what it's actually like to run and manage a real business. The stress of compliance, the chaos of a busy team, the frustration of software that was clearly built by people who've never had to use it themselves.

It's his mum who has been closest to SmartCore since the beginning — the one who asks the hard questions, gives honest feedback, and makes sure the product is built around how businesses actually operate, not how a developer imagines they do.

The real test

If it couldn't pass the "would a real business actually use this?" test, it didn't ship. That standard is still the bar everything is measured against.

What it means

More than a platform.
Something personal.

SmartCore isn't a side project. It isn't a portfolio piece. It's a genuine attempt to build something that matters — a platform that UK businesses can rely on without needing an IT department, a consultant, or a six-figure implementation budget.

For Finley, it's also proof of something. That you don't have to come from a big city tech scene to build something solid. That you don't need VC money or a co-founder from a famous company. That a person from Derbyshire, who started by exploring settings on gaming machines, can build infrastructure that businesses stake their operations on.

"Every module in SmartCore was built the same way I approached everything else — by understanding it completely before touching it. I'm not interested in building something that looks good in a demo. I'm interested in building something that works at 7am on a Monday morning when your team is already out on site." — Finley Hassall, Founder & Lead Developer, SmartCore Technology
How it's built

Hand-crafted code.
Enterprise-grade infrastructure.

SmartCore is built on two things that don't usually sit together: genuine hand-craftsmanship and serious cloud infrastructure. Every screen, every flow, every data structure was written by hand — not pulled from a template, not stitched together from components someone else made. That means the code is clean, the architecture is deliberate, and there's no dead weight buried inside it.

Underneath that craftsmanship sits infrastructure that can take the load. Encrypted data, redundant backups, real-time sync across devices, 99.8% uptime — the kind of backbone that keeps things running quietly while your business focuses on the actual work.
